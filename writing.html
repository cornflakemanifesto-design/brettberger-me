Excerpt: work in progress

The following is an excerpt from a work in progress — a long-form humor piece in the voice of a 1970s BBC wildlife documentarian narrating a Greyhound bus trip from Colorado Springs to Denver.

7:15am I have arrived at the predetermined site to begin what is sure to be an interesting study. My arrival was timed to be 15 minutes early as per stated on my digital boarding pass for the Greyhound #0000 bus for a one hour and twenty-five minute trip to Denver, CO. The Tejón Park-n-Ride in Colorado Springs, Colorado is where my collection of data will begin.

For the sake of continuity in data collection and for Mother Science herself, I must admit for the record that my arrival time of 7:16am was actually one minute late from the requested arrival time per Greyhound corporate policy due to a frantic last-minute search for my 2/3's of a roll of peppermint Mentos as I was preparing to leave for the Tejón Park-n-Ride. I apologize for this reckless lapse in professional standards and I have gone ahead and taken myself down a peg. I am horrified at my behavior and I'll ruminate about my shortcomings for weeks to come.

7:19am Having parked my silver 2021 Toyota Corolla in the Tejón Park-n-Ride parking lot, I double and then triple check that my silver 2021 Toyota Corolla is locked and secured in a well-lit and easily observable parking spot in hopes of giving pause to the local population of feral vagrant (H. sapiens coloradus) from mucking about my silver 2021 Toyota Corolla.

7:21am After observing the nature of the Tejón Park-n-Ride, I quadruple check that I've locked my silver 2021 Toyota Corolla.

7:23am Parked directly to the SE of my silver 2021 Toyota Corolla stands bus #0000 destined for Denver, Colorado. A small group of the local fauna has already arrived and is waiting in a disorganized group of 8-10 individuals dressed in their Tuesday morning finest. Cautiously, I approach the proud Vanhool CX45 motorcoach in all her American diesel glory. My heart is filled with excitement and my lungs with carbon monoxide.

7:23am I begin to shuffle across the parking lot towards bus #0000 with my eyes looking firmly down at my intelligent cellular telephone whilst simultaneously averting direct eye contact with my subjects so as to not arouse suspicion of my scientific intentions. Averting direct eye contact also provides me with true American "street cred" (bonafides) and cultural authenticity to help me blend in with the local population of preoccupied people. I do fear that my decision to forego application of a temporary facial tattoo prosthetic might raise some (tattooed) eyebrows amongst the subjects of my study. I take personal responsibility for this unfortunate oversight and have taken myself down another peg.

7:25am I have managed to infiltrate the dissociated group of locals by carefully choosing a random spot on the north side of the congregation. I use my peripherals to observe the crowd while pretending to be "doom-scrolling" the maniacal morning utterances of the tribal chief who the masses unwisely reelected after running on a campaign primarily focused on his first term accomplishments of throwing his turds at the White House Press Corps whilst blowing his own horn for passing a test designed for the mentally defective. My rouse has thus far gone unnoticed and I am beginning to feel more confident in my subterfuge. While not an excuse for my prior folly regarding my non-adoption of local facial tattoo customs, I am pleased with my garment choice—a T-shirt with one word of text on the front showing my resigned tolerance of existence using the American colloquialism of "meh".

Etymological note "Meh" likely stems from a blend of Yiddish and pop culture. Lexicographers trace the earliest printed record to Alexander Harkavy's 1928 Yiddish-English-Hebrew Dictionary, which lists me or meh to denote indifference. Pop culture popularized the term in the 1990s, specifically via American documentarian and cartoonist Matt Groenig's critically acclaimed The Simpsons.

7:30am The bus Captain opens the bus door and declares that boarding has begun in a stern yet understated call of "all-aboard!". In a moment of weakness, I forget myself and regrettably compromise my guise by allowing a small grin to appear on my face due to the realization that my ears bore witness to a real-life bus Captain yell of "all-aboard!" as if I was in one of those old documentaries about traversing the country in what locals call the "good ol' days".

Note The "good ol' days" seems to be an amorphous, anachronistic and possibly non-existent time period which varies in length, period and actual placetime in history depending on who is doing the reminiscing. The connecting thread between the accounts of these mysterious days is that they are vaguely referential to a "simpler time" in which the subject insists that they didn't have any "trouble" before "they" got here. While I am hesitant to imply any conscious feelings of malice from these accounts, I would be remiss if I didn't take note of the potential racist undertones of these misrememberances. This is surely fertile ground for future studies given the Americans' notorious non-racist history and acceptance of diversity.

Upon hearing the boarding call, the scraggly crowd of travelers rushes forward towards the now-open portal into the diesel leviathan that is the Van Hool CX45 Motorcoach. The vehicle assigned to whisk us away on our journey north is the Van Hool CX45 Motorcoach which proudly measures in at a length of 13.8 meters and which has a wheelbase of 7.7 meters. The Van Hool CX45 Motorcoach has a capacity of 56 passengers which makes the crowd's fight for their desired seats seem strange given the fact that there are only 11 of us. Whether this is a result of subpar mathematical abilities, a penchant for confrontation due to the ingestion of methamphetamines or some potentially heritable gene for an underdeveloped posterior parietal cortex is a question for a future time and perhaps a different researcher.
